# Your Story

**Why this matters:** technical skill gets you considered. Being able to explain your own work gets you hired. Only you can fill this note in, and **it needs to be true**.

## 1. Tell me about yourself (about 60 seconds)

Use this shape: **now, then past, then why this role.**

> "I'm a Flutter developer who [what you do now]. I've built [your best project], where I [what you personally did]. I enjoy [what you care about], and I'm excited about this role because [something specific about the company]."

> [!TIP]
> **No big project yet?**
>
> Your `flutter_interview_lab` is real work. It has MVVM, repositories, tests at three levels, and seven state management approaches. If you've worked through it, you can honestly say that you built it as practice.

**My Tasks is a project you can honestly describe**, as long as you really typed it:

> "I built a task app to practise. It loads from a REST API, keeps an offline copy using shared_preferences, and handles loading, error and empty states. I first wrote it with Provider, then refactored it to MVVM with an abstract repository, so that I could swap in a fake. I wrote unit tests for the ViewModel, and widget tests for each state. A widget test even caught a bug I couldn't see by eye, where a colored container was hiding a ListTile's tap effect."

Say clearly that it's a practice project. Being honest about that is much better than having it found out later.

## 4. Common questions

| Question | How to answer |
|---|---|
| Why Flutter? | One codebase, control over every pixel, a fast dev cycle. Add your own reason |
| Why this company? | Mention something specific. Spend 15 minutes on their website and app beforehand |
| Your weakness? | A real one, and what you're doing about it |
| Salary expectations? | Know your range before you walk in |
| Where do you see yourself in five years? | Growing deeper, taking on more ownership |

## 5. When you don't know something

You won't know everything, and that's normal. **Never bluff.** Say:

> "I haven't used that directly. Here's what I'd expect, based on what I do know, and here's how I'd find out."

Interviewers respect this far more than a confident wrong answer.
